# The Shattered Crown — Campaign Beats

Draft 1, Oct 6, 2026. Written by Claude Code; Rob approved its key decisions the same day. Together with `docs/DESIGN.md`, this is the source of truth for the story. Scenes are drafted in Ink from these beats; when the story changes, change it here first.

**How to read this.** Each chapter lists its beats in order: what happens, the main choices, the checks and fights, and what it changes later. Exact numbers (DCs, XP, gold) are set when the scenes are built and tuned in playtests. Every monster named here is in SRD 5.2.1; everything else (people, places, the Unking) is original. All names are working names.

**Rules every scene follows** (from CLAUDE.md): second person, present tense, two to four short paragraphs per beat; failure moves the story forward and never dead-ends a quest; options the character doesn't qualify for stay hidden. Most fights are Moderate, bosses are High, and every authored fight gets a twist where it can: a hazard, an objective, reinforcements or a chance to parley.

## The secret history (for writers; players learn it piece by piece)

**The Unking.** Long before Caldmere had a name, something rose out of the deep places of the world and began to unmake it. Not a god or a devil, but an undoing: it unmakes names, oaths, memories and the walls between the planes. The old songs call it the Unking because it wore a crown of nothing and every king who faced it was forgotten.

**The binding.** The first queen, **Ysolde the Binder**, bound it beneath the hill where the capital Highcrown now stands. Three peoples helped: the druids of the **Old Circle**, the dwarf-smiths of **Emberdeep**, and the elves of the **Glass Court**. A prison of stone is not enough for a thing that unmakes stone, so they built a prison of promises. Every great house swore an oath of peace on one crown, the **Concord Crown**. The oaths are the bars of the cage, and the living monarch who wears the crown is the lock. The peace between the houses was never the point. It was the price.

**The lie that grew.** Over the centuries the truth became ceremony. Kings wore the crown and believed it was a symbol. Only the Old Circle remembered, and they dwindled. Thirty years ago **King Osric Caldwen** learned the truth from the last goliaths of the **Highvane** clan, who guarded the prison's mountain gate. Fearing the secret would tear the realm apart, he had the clan destroyed. One child survived.

**The shattering, twenty years ago.** The crown must pass from living head to living head. Osric lay dying, and his heir, **Princess Maelis**, was twelve. His steward, **Corvin Hale**, believed a child queen would be eaten alive by the great houses, and tried to take the crown "into keeping" until she came of age. They struggled over it in the king's chamber. The king died with the crown half off his head. With no living wearer, the lock failed for one heartbeat, and the crown shattered into **seven shards** that flew like sparks to the places where its oaths were first sworn.

In that heartbeat, the Unking spoke to the girl in the room, in her father's voice. Maelis ran into the night and everyone believed her dead. Hale became **Lord Regent** "until the crown is mended", and kept one secret: a shard landed at his feet that night, and he has hidden it ever since.

**Now.** The lock still holds, barely, because each shard rests where the binding remembers it. **Every shard taken from its resting place weakens the lock.** Maelis grew up among a sect of ash-robed mourners who sing the old coronation hymns, and she now leads them as **the Precentor of the Ashen Choir**. She believes her father is the "Sleeping King", asleep not dead, waiting for the crown to be reforged so he can wake. The voice in her dreams is the Unking wearing his memory. The Choir raises the dead to dig, because the old dead remember where the oaths were sworn.

## The seven shards

Each shard grants a power. Using a power raises the hidden **Whisper** (see below). A shard whispers to whoever carries it.

| # | Shard | Resting place | Found in | Power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Names | On the breast of Dame Aveline, the first queen's champion, in the Kings' Barrow | Ch 3 | Speak with the dead, and learn any living creature's name |
| 2 | Tides | The flooded crypt of Lowtide Abbey | Ch 5 | Breathe water; calm or raise the sea |
| 3 | the Anvil | The heart of Emberdeep's Great Forge | Ch 6 | For one round nothing can break you; or shatter any metal |
| 4 | Masks | The Mirror Pool of the Glass Court | Ch 7 | Wear any face |
| 5 | Wings | Vharkessa's hoard | Ch 8 | Flight |
| 6 | Sight | The Lord Regent's locked reliquary in Highcrown | Ch 10–11 | Foresight: see the next roll (advantage), and glimpse traps and ambushes |
| 7 | Command | Worn on a chain by the Precentor | Ch 9–13 | A terrible word of command that many must obey |

If you fail to secure a shard in Act II, a faction takes it instead (failure moves forward): the Act III war is then partly about getting it back. All seven come together at the end of Act III.

## The Whisper

A hidden score from 0 to 100. It rises when you use a shard's power, when a dark option tempts you and you take it, and a little with every shard you carry through a long rest. Old Circle rites (taught in the Interlude) lower it a little, but never to zero.

- **25+:** the DM describes a voice in your rests. Dark choice cards begin to appear, such as "Unmake the guard's memory of you".
- **50+:** companions notice; some approval reactions change; frightened NPCs call you "crown-touched".
- **75+:** the **Kneel** ending opens. The Unking now speaks to you openly in your Bond's voice.

## The betrayal (Act III, Chapter 12)

Someone you trusted steals the shards and opens the way into the Undercity, believing they're saving the realm. Who it is depends on your choices. Check in order, and the first match is the betrayer:

1. The companion with the lowest approval, if it's below the "leaving" line.
2. Your rival (Rival Bond only), if you never reconciled.
3. The leader of the faction you backed, if you also made a secret deal with another faction.
4. Otherwise, Lord Regent Hale (the Shard of Sight shows him a vision of you betraying him first). If you backed the Regency, it's Magister Venn instead.

## The endings (Act IV, Chapter 16)

As DESIGN.md sets out, with what each needs:

1. **Wear the crown.** Needs all seven shards. You reforge it at the First Forge and become the lock: ruler forever, never free.
2. **Crown a ruler.** Needs all seven shards and a living, willing candidate from the faction you backed:
   - **Regency:** Lord Regent Hale.
   - **Ashen Choir:** Maelis. If you turned her, she chooses to be the lock knowingly, a tragic rightful queen. If not, she wears it still listening to the voice, which is the grim version.
   - **Free League:** Magister Venn, as "First Citizen".
   - **Old Circle:** Hesper. She will die within years, and the Circle must find the next keeper.
3. **Break the prison.** Destroy the crown and fight the Unking. This is the hardest fight in the game. How many allies come depends on what you earned across the acts: the goblins, Emberdeep, the Glass Court, the merfolk, Vharkessa, the factions and loyal companions. With too few it can't be won, and Fate's Mercy turns the loss into a bleak but survivable epilogue.
4. **Kneel.** Needs a Whisper of 75 or more. You bargain with the Unking, open the last lock yourself and take the realm.

The epilogue then shows each town, faction, companion and your Bond, built from story flags and deeds.

# Act I · The Barrow Dead (levels 1–5, about 6 hours)

**Act goal:** find out why the dead are walking out of the barrows, rescue the miller, stop the Ashen Choir in the Kings' Barrow, and end holding the first shard.
**Fate's Mercy in Act I:** you wake in the Temple of the Steadfast Flame (Odda found you), robbed of your coin; or as a Choir prisoner in a barrow ossuary, with an escape scene.

## Chapter 1 · Bramblegate (levels 1 → 3, about 2 hours)

**The Phase 1 vertical slice is beats 1–7** (about an hour, Fighter or Wizard, solo). Phase 2 adds the side content at the end of the chapter.

**Before beat 1: character creation.** The opening line ties to your Drive: "You came north for glory / for your faith / for coin / for answers…" You've come because the reeve posted a call for capable folk: the dead are walking, and goblins are raiding.

1. **The north gate.** *(This is the Phase 0 test scene, expanded.)* Rain at dusk; the gate is barred; Warden Tamsin Pike won't open it.
   - **Choices:** Persuasion, Intimidation or Deception. Soldier background: show your regiment's token, and the gate opens with no roll. Acolyte: ask for sanctuary. Criminal: climb the wall (Athletics). Wizard: a cantrip trick with the lantern. Or wait for dawn.
   - **Failing or waiting:** you shelter under the eaves and see pale lights on the barrow hills (`saw_barrow_light`).
   - It teaches the first d20.
2. **Morning at the Tallow and Thistle.** Morwen Tallow brings breakfast and gossip: goblins hit the mill last night, and Garrick Dunn the miller is gone. The town opens up: the inn, the temple, Hob's forge, the market, the reeve's hall and the notice board.
   - Reeve Corbin's bounty: find the miller, end the raids, 50 gp. Captain Varrow adds "ten silver a goblin ear."
   - **Lark Dunn** corners you: "Goblins don't *take* people. Something else did."
   - **Drive moments:** every Drive gets one here. Take the job for the silver (Wealth), because nobody should vanish (Justice), or for the story they'll tell (Glory); pray for the miller at the temple (Faith); quiz Hob about the barrows (Knowledge); tear down a runaway's wanted notice (Freedom); promise Lark you'll bring her father home (Kinship).
   - It teaches shopping and the journal. The market sells cheap gear, and Potions of Healing at 50 gold, which few new heroes can afford, so Morwen gives every hero one on the house ("I'm not baking with gravel").
   - Small seeds: Hob's cousin saw goblins fleeing the barrow hills; Prior Crane flinches at "Kings' Barrow" (Insight); a lost cat called Biscuit on the notice board (Too Many Cats, Phase 2).
3. **Dunn's Mill.** Flour drifts across the step like snow.
   - **Checks:** Investigation (goblin feet *and* human boots, one set dragging) and Perception (cold grey ash, grave dirt).
   - **Fighter:** "The door was barred from inside and broken *outward*." **Wizard:** "The ash marks a burned sigil: a staff of music notes." **Dwarf:** "This cellar floor is hollow, with old dressed stone underneath."
   - **Discovery:** the cellar floor has been broken through from below into an old tunnel, now choked with rubble.
   - **First fight (the combat tutorial, Low):** two goblin scavengers (two Goblin Minions: 50 XP, a Low fight for one level 1 hero by the 2024 budget; the Goblin Warrior waits in the warren) come back for more flour. Fight them, talk them down (Persuasion), or roar them off (Intimidation). They're starving, and Mother Nettle sent them. Talking takes you straight to Nettle and skips the warren's outer fights; scaring them off leaves a flour trail to follow. Losing the fight is Fate's Mercy: you wake in the temple, robbed, where Lark carried you.
4. **The quarry road.** A short trip to the old quarry. A Wolf worries at a goblin corpse covered in claw marks that no wolf made (a Low fight, or drive it off with Animal Handling or a loud noise).
5. **Brackenhollow warren** *(the slice's dungeon, 6 rooms)*:
   1. **Quarry mouth:** a goblin lookout. Sneak past (Stealth), parley, or fight.
   2. **Spike-pit passage:** a trap (Perception to spot it, Dexterity save). The goblins' traps all face *inward*: they're afraid of something coming from below.
   3. **The larder:** empty apart from the miller's flour sacks, and a goblin child hiding: "Not food! Not *food!*" Spare it or scare it, and Nettle hears either way.
   4. **Nettle's hall:** Mother Nettle with three Goblin Warriors and some Minions, in a standoff.
      - **Parley (Persuasion or Insight):** the dead broke into the lower warren two nights ago, and the goblins raid because their stores are down there. They saw grey-robed singers drag a man through the tunnels.
      - **The deal:** "Clear the dead from our lower warren and we stop raiding." This is a Freedom or Kinship Drive moment.
      - **Or fight:** the whole band is far beyond even a High fight for one hero, so Nettle offers single combat with her instead (High). Or take the captain's way and wipe them out, the hard way.
      - **Flags:** `goblins_spared` or `goblins_slain`.
   5. **The lower warren:** two Zombies dig mindlessly at the walls (Moderate). Twist: they're digging *away* from you, and attack only if disturbed.
   6. **The breach:** the warren breaks into ancient dressed stone, part of the barrow tunnels. A Choir acolyte (Cultist) sings a Skeleton to its feet. The fight's objective is to stop the hymn before a second Skeleton rises.
      - **If you catch the acolyte:** he talks under Intimidation or Persuasion: "The Precentor will wake the Sleeping King. The miller works the gates."
      - **If he escapes:** he drops his hymnal. Either way you get the hymnal: coronation hymns, a rough map of the barrow road and the Choir's sigil (`found_hymnal`).
   - **Treasure:** the goblin hoard is shiny junk plus a Potion of Healing and a goblin-tooth charm (original trinket).
6. **Back to Bramblegate.** Reeve Corbin pays.
   - **Captain Varrow** approves or disapproves of how you handled the goblins, which sets the town's attitude.
   - If you spared them, Nettle's band camps by the mill and trades (a later ally).
   - **Morwen** reads the hymnal: "The Ashen Choir. They sing at funerals that aren't theirs."
   - **Prior Crane** goes pale when he sees it (foreshadowing).
   - **Long rest at the inn:** reach level 3, and choose a subclass (Champion or Evoker).
7. **Lights on the hills.** *(End of the slice; a natural stopping point.)* From the inn window, pale lanterns move on the barrow hills. Lark knocks: "They're digging toward the Kings' Barrow."

**Levels:** the first fights, the mill and the goblin deal take you to level 2. The warren, the breach and the report take you to level 3. Quest and discovery XP fill the gap, so the fights stay few and good.

**Chapter 1 side content** (Phase 2):
- **Too Many Cats** (side quest): the inn's cats keep vanishing. A Stirge nest in the loft. Funny, short, and a good first loot.
- **Wolf den on the north road** (the first contract on the notice board): Wolves; or find out they're starving because the dead drove off the deer.
- **Hob's bet** (social): the smith bets you can't out-arm-wrestle the smithy's apprentice (an Athletics contest). Win a discount; lose and buy the next round.

## Chapter 2 · The Barrow Road (levels 3 → 4, about 2 hours)

1. **The miller's missing daughter.** *(The side-quest example from DESIGN.md, now part of the main line.)* At dawn Lark went down the mill tunnel with a lantern and her father's hatchet, and the dead have dug it clear. A short delve (4 rooms): Ghouls in the old ossuary, and Lark cornered on a ledge. You rescue her. She becomes a recurring face, and wants to be your apprentice.
2. **Odda Brasswick arrives.** A dwarf priest sent by her order's mother-house to look into the walking dead: brusque, kind, and always brewing tea. She helps with the rescue and joins. *(Companion.)*
3. **Fen in the stocks.** A halfling pickpocket, caught with the reeve's purse. Bounty hunters arrive (a Bandit Captain and two Bandits): "Dead or alive. The Choir pays double for dead."
   - Fen begs: "I know where they're taking your miller. I used to sing with them."
   - **Choices:**
     - Free him: pick the lock, persuade Varrow, or pay his fine. He joins. *(Companion.)*
     - Hand him over (a Wealth Drive moment).
     - Fight the hunters (Moderate).
   - Turning Fen away doesn't dead-end anything: Nettle's goblins or the hunters give up the same lead, at a price.
4. **The Bond returns** *(the Act I slot; see the Bond table)*.
5. **The Old Ford.** At night, the dead cross the ford carrying capstones from the barrows. The watch-post there is under siege (Zombies and a Ghoul; the objective is to keep three soldiers alive).
   - **Clue:** the dead are unsealing the old oath-graves one by one. Each was a ward.
   - Saving the soldiers raises Regency approval, and the Sibling Bond's letter came from this post.
6. **The temple's secret.** *(Odda's personal hook.)* Prior Crane's ledger shows a payment from "the Choir of Ash." He sold the temple's centuries-old map of the Kings' Barrow to clear the temple's debts, owed to Free League moneylenders.
   - **Choices:**
     - Expose him to the reeve (Justice).
     - Let Odda decide: she asks for a public confession (Faith or Kinship).
     - Blackmail him (Wealth).
     - Help him make it right: he gives you the barrow's ward-word, useful in Chapter 3.
   - Odda's approval swings hard on this.
7. **The camp at Cairnfield.** The Choir's camp among the opened barrows: acolytes (Cultists), a Cultist Fanatic called Sister Rhosyn, and the walking dead.
   - **Approaches:**
     - Sneak in (Stealth, with Fen's help).
     - Blend in: Fen knows the hymns, so it's a Performance check to sing along.
     - Attack.
   - **What you learn:** Cantor Vesk opens the barrow's crown vault at the dark of the moon, three days away. The miller is alive, working the sluice gates that drain the barrow's flooded lower vault.
8. **Eve of the barrow.** A camp scene with Odda and Fen: companion conversations and a long rest. You reach level 4 (Ability Score Improvement or feat).

**Side content:**
- **The Hanging Tree** (Fen): an old Choir dead-drop with a coded note about the Precentor.
- **Hob's ore** (contract): escort a cart through Gnoll country.
- Road events on the barrow road.

## Chapter 3 · The Kings' Barrow (levels 4 → 5, about 2 hours)

1. **Under the dark moon.** The allies you earned turn up, or don't:
   - Nettle's goblins cause a diversion (if spared).
   - Varrow's watch holds the outer cairns (if you saved the Old Ford).
   - The ward-word (if Crane gave it).
   - Your rival (Rival Bond).
2. **The barrow gate.** Two Animated Armors guard it (Low).
   - Speak the ward-word, or pass a Religion or History check, and they kneel.
   - **Dwarf:** "This sealing stone is dwarf-cut. It pivots."
3. **The Hall of Champions.** The tombs of the first queen's sworn champions.
   - A pressure-plate dart corridor.
   - **The oath-wall:** read the champions' oaths to open the way (History or Arcana, with a bonus for a Sage). Their names matter later in this chapter.
   - **Temptation:** loot the graves (Wealth). Odda dislikes it, and it raises the Whisper.
4. **The flooded vault.** Garrick the miller works a great sluice wheel under Choir guard. An Ogre Zombie wades in the black water (a Moderate fight; the hazard is deep water).
   - Free the miller, then choose:
     - Drain the vault, which opens the quick way down.
     - Flood it back, which cuts off Choir reinforcements but means the long way round.
5. **The Chantry of Ash.** Cantor Vesk sings the dead awake: a Cultist Fanatic, plus a Wight (the risen champion, Dame Aveline) and Zombies (High, the boss fight).
   - **The twist:** call the Wight by her name from the oath-wall, and she stops, remembers her oath, and turns on Vesk.
   - **Flag:** `aveline_rested` or `aveline_destroyed`.
6. **The first shard.** On Aveline's breastplate lies a sliver of gold that hums. As Vesk dies he breaks its seal, and it flies to your hand.
   - It whispers your name: your full name, the one your Bond calls you.
   - A vision: a girl in a burning chamber, and a crown breaking.
   - The barrow shudders as if something far away shifted in its sleep (`shard_names`, Whisper begins).
7. **Bramblegate's thanks.** A feast at the Tallow and Thistle, the miller home, Lark beaming, the reeve's reward.
   - A Regency envoy, **Knight-Captain Edda Marchbank**, "requests" you bring the shard to Highcrown.
   - A Free League courier slips you a letter.
   - An old woman with an owl watches from the crowd (the Old Circle).
   - That night the shard says your name again. You reach level 5. **End of Act I.**

**Side content:**
- **The Barrow-Wives** (side quest): three restless Specters in the lesser barrows, each laid to rest by finding what they lost.
- **The Tollman's Lantern** (road event): a Will-o'-Wisp on the marsh road (dangerous at this level, best avoided).

# Act II · The Shard Road (levels 5–10, about 15 hours)

**Act goal:** recover the four shards in the realm's corners before the Choir does, while three factions court you and the Choir hunts you, and learn what the crown really is.
**Fate's Mercy in Act II:** you wake in a faction's custody, owing them (a faction-debt flag), or robbed of a shard (it goes to the Choir).

## Chapter 4 · Three Offers (levels 5 → 6, about 2.5 hours)

1. **The road south.** Knight-Captain Marchbank escorts you, together with **Brakka Holloway**, an orc sellsword assigned to "protect" you (to watch you). Brakka is gruff and loyal to her word, and joins. *(Companion.)*
2. **The toll-bridge trolls.** Two Trolls run a bridge toll. Fighting them is deadly at this level, so it's a social encounter. The journal line: "Day 31: talked the toll-bridge trolls down to half price."
3. **The Choir's ambush.** Cultist Fanatics and Shadows on a lonely stretch. The Precentor speaks through a corpse: "Bring it to me, and I'll give back what you lost." The Unking is already learning your Bond.
4. **The three offers:**
   - **Regency:** a writ, gold and a title "for the shard's safe keeping".
   - **Free League:** Magister Venn's agent, **Pell Marrick**, offers ship passage, money and freedom.
   - **Old Circle:** Hesper's owl leads you to a stone ring at night: "Every shard you lift loosens a chain."
   - You can accept, refuse or play them against each other. That faction standing shapes the act.
5. **The shard calls to its siblings.** It shows you four places, which unlocks the region map: the Drowned Abbey, Emberdeep, the Glass Court and Vharkessa's mountain. You choose the order.

## Chapter 5 · The Drowned Abbey (any order)

- **The place:** **Lowtide Abbey** sank in a storm on the night of the shattering, dragged under as the shard arrived. Only at the low spring tide does its spire show, and the drowned monks still walk its cloisters.
- **The pressure:** the Free League wants the Tides shard for its fleets, and Pell Marrick's smugglers use the sea-caves.
- **The encounters:**
  - Sahuagin raiders.
  - Drowned dead (Zombies and Ghouls).
  - A Sea Hag who trades in memories.
  - Merfolk who become allies if you help them.
- **The deadline:** the tide comes back in. Each scene costs time.
- **The climax:** the flooded crypt. The drowned abbess (a Ghost) guards the shard, and a Water Elemental is bound to it. You can lay her to rest with the Shard of Names, or fight.

## Chapter 6 · Emberdeep (any order)

- **The place:** the dwarven foundry-hold where the crown's gold was first worked. The shard fell into the Great Forge, which has burned out of control ever since, and the lower halls are sealed.
- **The pressure:** dwarf politics. **Thane Hulda Ironmantle** wants the forge back, the forge-masters want it sealed for good, and the Regency wants Emberdeep's weapons for the coming war.
- **The encounters:** Azers, Magmins, Salamanders, a Fire Elemental and Stone Golem forge-guardians.
- **Odda's personal quest:** her order was founded in Emberdeep by a dwarf who helped bind the Unking. Her faith is shaken, then renewed (or not, depending on approval).

## Chapter 7 · The Glass Court (any order)

- **The place:** an elven court in the Whitewood, where time runs strangely. For twenty years the court's queen, **Aelith**, has used the shard from the Mirror Pool to wear any face. Nobody is sure what her real one looks like.
- **Ithrien Vale:** an exiled elf scholar, joins at the court's edge *(companion)*. She was banished for studying the crown, and she hid her findings in the court archive.
- **The masked ball:** an intrigue chapter. Find the true queen among masked courtiers (Insight), steal the archive (Stealth), or win the queen's favour (Performance or Persuasion).
- **The encounters:** Dryads, Satyrs, Sprites, a Green Hag in the briars and a Treant at the court's gate.
- **Fey bargains:** they always have prices, which become flags that come due in Act III.

## Chapter 8 · Vharkessa's Hoard (any order)

- **The place:** the mountain of **Vharkessa**, an old Adult Red Dragon whose wings are failing. She wants the Shard of Wings for herself, and you can't fight her at this level and win.
- **Lucan Morrow:** a tiefling warlock, is here to steal the shard for his patron. He joins *(companion)*, or becomes a rival.
- **Approaches:**
  - A heist: Stealth past her Young Red Dragon brood, and the Gargoyles and Hell Hounds in the mountain halls.
  - A bargain: what will she take for it?
  - An alliance: promise her a war to fly in, and she becomes a mighty Act III ally.
  - A fight: possible only with every advantage, or she simply lets you flee.

## Interlude · The Ring of Nine (after any two shards)

- **The truth:** the Old Circle's moot in a ring of standing stones. Hesper Wildmoor tells you what the crown truly holds: the lock, the oaths and the Unking. Ithrien's hidden research, if she's with you, confirms it.
- **The proof:** omens have grown with every shard you've lifted. Dead birds fall silent mid-song, borders blur on old maps, and the sea runs backwards for an hour.
- **The rite:** she teaches a rite that lowers the Whisper a little.
- **The dilemma:** keep gathering shards to keep them from the Choir, which weakens the lock; or return one to rest, which strengthens it but leaves that shard for the Choir to take. Neither is wrong.

## Chapter 9 · The Summons (level 10, about 2 hours)

1. **Omens and a summons.** The Regent summons you to Highcrown, firmly.
2. **Bramblegate besieged.** The Choir marches on the town you saved: Lark, Morwen, Nettle, Varrow. It's a big set-piece defence, and every Act I choice pays off here.
3. **The Precentor unmasked.** Maelis walks out of the smoke and lowers her hood: the lost princess. She calls the great houses to her. If she can, she takes a shard from you (failure moves forward).
4. **The realm splits.** Houses declare for Maelis, the Free League declares its independence, and the Regent declares war. **End of Act II.**

# Act III · The Crown War (levels 11–16, about 15 hours)

**Act goal:** survive the civil war, choose who (if anyone) should hold the crown, and gather all seven shards. Someone you trust will betray you.
**Fate's Mercy in Act III:** you're captured by the side you fought, with a trial or ransom scene, or you lose a companion's trust.

## Chapter 10 · Highcrown (levels 11 → 12, about 3.5 hours)

- **The capital:** Highcrown is built on the hill above the prison. It has the Regent's court, the Free League's embassy, Choir agents in every crowd, and nobles changing sides daily.
- **Tor Highvane:** a goliath wanderer, arrives seeking the truth about his clan, or the end of the Caldwen line. He joins *(companion)*, or hunts Maelis on his own.
- **The Regent's secret:** discover the Shard of Sight in Hale's reliquary through a heist (Fen), a scrying (Ithrien), or his confession if he trusts you.
- **Choose your side:** Regency, Ashen Choir, Free League, Old Circle, or reach for the crown yourself. It's possible to play several at once, and dangerous.

## Chapter 11 · The War of Shards (levels 12 → 14, about 4 hours)

- **Three operations:** chosen by your side, such as the siege of a Free League port, a second battle at the Old Ford, or the relief of Emberdeep.
- **Allies arrive:** the goblins of Brackenhollow, the dwarves, the Glass Court, the merfolk and Vharkessa.
- **Companion personal quests come to a head:**
  - Brakka's brother in the salt mines.
  - Lucan's patron calls in its price.
  - Fen faces the Precentor over the stolen ledger.
  - Tor confronts the Caldwen legacy.
- **King Osric's tomb:** with the Shard of Names, ask the dead king himself. His answer proves the "Sleeping King" isn't him. This is the key to turning Maelis.

## Chapter 12 · The Betrayal (levels 14 → 15, about 3.5 hours)

1. **The truce council.** All factions meet at the Concord Stone in Highcrown to decide the crown's fate. It's a talky, tense chapter opening where your faction standings matter.
2. **The betrayal.** Chosen by the rules above. The betrayer seizes the shards and breaks the seal beneath the palace, opening the **Undercity**.
3. **The Bond's Act III beat.**
4. **Pursuit.** Down into the dark after them.

## Chapter 13 · The Undercity (levels 15 → 16, about 4 hours)

- **The place:** the first queen's buried city, built over the prison's lid, with wards failing.
- **The encounters:**
  - The founders' guardians: a Mummy Lord and Clay and Iron Golems.
  - Things the Unking has half-unmade: Shadows, Wraiths and Specters.
  - An Aboleth that has slept in the prison's cistern since before the binding.
- **The First Forge:** where the crown was made, and the only place it can be reforged.
- **Maelis:** if you turned her, she stands with you; if not, she reaches the prison gate first.
- **The climax:** the seven shards come together, and the betrayer is dealt with (killed, spared, redeemed). The strain cracks the lock wide open. The prison opens, and rifts tear across Caldmere. **End of Act III.**

# Act IV · What Sleeps Beneath (levels 17–20, about 10 hours)

**Act goal:** go down into the prison and decide what the crown really was.
**Fate's Mercy in Act IV:** the Unking spits you back out, unmade a little. You lose a memory (a deed is erased from your journal) and part of your name.

## Chapter 14 · The Fraying (level 17, about 3 hours)

- **The rifts:** the walls between planes fail across the realm. There are fire rifts at Emberdeep (Efreeti and Fire Elementals), a drowned rift at Lowtide (a Kraken stirs), and fiends at Highcrown (Vrocks, Glabrezus and a Horned Devil).
- **Choose what to save:** you can hold only two of the four regions. The others suffer, and the epilogue remembers.
- **Last conversations:** final companion camp scenes. The allies you earned gather for the descent.

## Chapter 15 · The Descent (levels 18 → 19, about 4 hours)

- **The prison's layers:**
  - The Hall of Broken Oaths, where every oath ever broken takes shape.
  - The Drowned Dark.
  - The Furnace, where a Balor was bound long ago as a jailer.
  - The Hollow Court, where the first queen's court wizard went mad keeping watch, now a Lich who believes he still guards the lock.
- **Lucan's patron:** the Hollow Ledger makes its final move.
- **The first queen's ghost:** she tells you how the binding was truly made. You reach level 19 and gain an Epic Boon.

## Chapter 16 · The Unking (level 20, about 3 hours)

- **The heart:** a throne room of nothing. The Unking speaks to you in your Bond's voice.
- **The decision:** the four endings, each with its own final trial:
  - **Wear the crown:** a trial of will as the lock closes around you.
  - **Crown a ruler:** hold the Unking back while your chosen ruler is crowned.
  - **Break the prison:** the hardest fight in the game, with every ally you earned.
  - **Kneel:** the bargain, and what your companions do when you make it.
- **The epilogue:** each town, faction, companion and your Bond, told from your flags and deeds.

## Key decisions (approved by Rob, Oct 6, 2026)

The biggest calls in this draft, all approved as written. Changing one later means updating this file first.

1. **Maelis is the lost princess and the Precentor.** It ties the villain to the crown's history and gives the Choir a sympathetic, tragic face.
2. **The Regent caused the shattering and secretly holds a shard.**
3. **Seven shards:** five found on the Shard Road, plus two held by the Regent and the Precentor.
4. **Act II's four shard chapters can be played in any order,** with fights sized to your level.
5. **The betrayer is chosen by rules** (lowest approval, then rival, then double-crossed faction, then a default).
6. **Companion working names:** Odda, Fen, Brakka, Ithrien, Lucan, Tor.
7. **The capital is called Highcrown.**
